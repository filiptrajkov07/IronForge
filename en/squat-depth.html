Squat depth, explained by our coaches

How low you should go, why your heels lift off the floor, and three drills that make a deeper squat feel normal within a few weeks.

Strength Marko Petrovski 28 Sep 2026 6 min read

How deep is deep enough

For most people the answer is parallel: the crease of your hip drops to the height of the top of your knee. That is also the standard judges use in powerlifting. Go lower if you can keep your back flat and your heels down. Stop higher if you can't, and work on the drills below.

Depth matters because the bottom of the squat is where your glutes and adductors do most of the work. Half squats load the knees and quads and leave a lot of strength on the table.

“Take 20 kg off the bar and add 10 cm of depth. You'll get stronger faster.”

Why your heels lift

Usually it is the ankles. If your shins can't travel forward far enough, your body finds the depth somewhere else, and the heels come up. Three quick fixes:

Widen your stance a little and turn your toes out 15–30°. Sit between your hips, not behind them. And if your ankles are stiff, squat with your heels on a 2–3 cm plate while you work on mobility.

Three drills

Goblet squat hold Hold a dumbbell at your chest and sit at the bottom. Push your knees out with your elbows.
3 × 30 s
Box squat to parallel Set a box at parallel. Sit back to touch it, pause, stand up. The box teaches you where depth is.
3 × 8
Ankle rocks Half-kneel facing a wall and drive your front knee past your toes, heel down. Ten each side.
2 × 10

A 4-week plan

Do the drills before your normal squat sessions, two or three times a week.

Week 1 · Goblet holds, light box squats 3 × 8 · 60%
Week 2 · Box squats with a pause 4 × 6 · 65%
Week 3 · Free squats to parallel 4 × 5 · 70%
Week 4 · Technique check with a coach 5 × 3 · 75%

In short: aim for parallel, keep your heels down, and lower the weight before you shorten the squat.
